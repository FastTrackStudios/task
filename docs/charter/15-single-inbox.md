# A Single Inbox - For All Things

_Charter #15 (formerly issue #15)._

One place everything unprocessed lands — regardless of whether it arrived as an email, a file someone dropped, a voice note, a message, an agent's suggestion, or a thought you had in the car.

The test is simple: if there is any category of incoming thing that has its own separate queue you have to remember to check, the inbox has failed.

Prompts worth answering: What can enter the inbox, and by what routes — capture, email, file-request upload, share invitation, agent proposal, watch dictation, someone else's assignment? Is an inbox item a copy, a pointer, or the thing itself? What does processing an item produce — task, event, note, project, file, or nothing? What does zero mean, and is it reachable? What happens to items you neither act on nor delete? Which arrivals may an agent pre-triage, and what must always reach a human untouched? How does the inbox behave offline, and across devices? What does the inbox owe you when it has 400 items — order, grouping, batching, or something else?

## Current state

A capture inbox exists with capture / list / process operations, and captures arrive as suggestions the user accepts. But the queues are not yet unified: email sits in its own read-and-draft surface, file-request uploads land in a per-token incoming area awaiting promotion, share invitations have their own incoming list, and agent output arrives through chat rather than the inbox.

That fragmentation is precisely what this charter exists to close.
