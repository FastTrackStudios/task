# The Ideal Fitness Tracker

_Charter #24 (formerly issue #24)._

Training, movement and the body — tracked well enough to actually change behaviour, and honest enough that a bad month reads as a bad month rather than a broken streak.

## Hard requirements

_To be detailed._

Prompts worth answering: What is the unit — a workout, a session, a set, a movement? How is a program expressed, and what happens when you miss a week of it? How much can be captured automatically (watch, phone, gym equipment) versus typed, and what is the minimum manual entry that still yields useful data? What comes in from other systems — Apple Health, a watch, a heart-rate strap — and is that a copy or a pointer? What does progressive overload need the system to remember? How does recovery, sleep and soreness factor in without becoming pseudo-science? What does it show you mid-set versus at the end of a block? What does the phone and watch experience need with sweaty hands and no attention to spare? What does it never do — guilt, streaks, comparison to other people?

## The test

Same standard as [An Ideal Way to Spend Your Time](23-spending-your-time.md): a program you abandon in week two is worse than none, because it teaches you not to trust the system. Requirements are judged on whether they survive an interrupted, imperfect training block.
