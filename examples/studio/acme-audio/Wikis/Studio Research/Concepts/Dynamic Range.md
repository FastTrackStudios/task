# Dynamic Range

The distance between the quietest and loudest parts of a signal that
still carry information. In a mix it is what makes a chorus feel like an
arrival rather than a continuation: the ear reads *change* in level, and
a passage with nowhere to go reads as flat however loud it is.

## Why the meter and the ear disagree

Peak level says how close the signal is to clipping; it says almost
nothing about how loud the result sounds. Two masters at the same peak
can be a long way apart perceptually, because the ear integrates over
time rather than sampling instants. This is why a master that measures
"louder" often sounds *smaller* — the loudness was bought by removing
the range that made the loud parts read as loud.

## What it costs to spend it

Compression trades range for density, and the trade is not free in both
directions: range spent is hard to get back, because what was removed
was the difference between two passages and nothing restores a
difference. The practical rule is the same one that governs
[[Equalization]] — subtract deliberately, and know what you spent.

The commercial pressure that made this a fight rather than a choice is
its own subject; see [[Loudness War]].

## Open

How much of the perceived-loudness effect survives streaming
normalisation is not settled here — the platforms normalise to a target
now, which changes the incentive but not obviously the practice.
