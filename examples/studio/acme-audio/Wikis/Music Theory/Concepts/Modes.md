# Modes

Seven rotations of the major scale, each starting on a different degree
and each with its own characteristic interval. The mode is not the
notes — every mode of C major uses the same seven pitches — it is which
note the music treats as home.

| mode | starts on | characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| [[Ionian]] | 1 | major 7th |
| Dorian | 2 | major 6th over a minor 3rd |
| Phrygian | 3 | flat 2nd |
| Lydian | 4 | sharp 4th |
| Mixolydian | 5 | flat 7th |
| Aeolian | 6 | natural minor |
| Locrian | 7 | flat 5th |

## Why a mode holds

A mode needs something to hold the tonic in place — a drone, a pedal,
or a bass part that keeps returning. Without it the ear reinterprets
the material as the parent major scale, which is why modal parts often
collapse the moment the bass moves.
