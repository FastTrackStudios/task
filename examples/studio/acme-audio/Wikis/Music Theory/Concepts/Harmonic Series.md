# Harmonic Series

A vibrating string or air column does not produce one frequency. It
produces a fundamental and a series of whole-number multiples of it,
called partials.

The relative strength of the partials is most of what we hear as
timbre — it is why a cello and a clarinet playing the same written
pitch are instantly distinguishable. ^partials

## Why it matters at the desk

Two instruments an octave apart share most of their partials, so they
compete for the same spectrum even though a musician would call them
different registers. That is the theory under the engineering advice in
[[acme.test/audio-production::Equalization@2026-09-01|Equalization]].

The first six partials outline a major triad, which is why
[[acme.test/music-theory::Modes@2026-09-01|modal]] writing over a drone
sounds consonant even without a chord instrument playing.
