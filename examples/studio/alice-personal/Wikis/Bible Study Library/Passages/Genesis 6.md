# Genesis 6

Four verses ([[bible::Gen.6.1-Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:1–4]]) that set up the flood — and, on one reading, much of what follows in Canaan.

## The sons of God

[[Bene Elohim|bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm]], “sons of God”, is the phrase [[bible::Job.38.7|Job 38:7]] uses for the heavenly beings present at creation. Read the same way here, members of the [[Divine Council]] cross into the human world and take wives, and their offspring are the Nephilim, “the mighty men of old” [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1374|22:54]].

That was the dominant ancient Jewish reading (1 Enoch and other Second Temple texts) and is assumed in the background of [[bible::1Pet.3.19-1Pet.3.20|1 Peter 3:19–20]], [[bible::2Pet.2.4|2 Peter 2:4]] and [[bible::Jude.1.6|Jude 1:6]]. Other readings — the line of Seth marrying the line of Cain, or dynastic kings — came later: the Sethite reading first appears in Jewish sources in the mid-2nd century AD, and the early church fathers read the passage as divine beings [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2277|37:57]]. *1 Enoch*’s fuller story — the watchers on Mount Hermon, and demons as the spirits of their dead children — is on [[Watchers and Demons]].

This is the second of [[Three Rebellions]]; the sons of God here are not the ones later given the nations [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2232|37:12]].

## “And also afterward”

[[bible::Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:4]] places the Nephilim on the earth “in those days, and also afterward”. The flood is judgment on what the rebellion produced, but it does not end the line: the spies meet their descendants in [[bible::Num.13.33|Numbers 13:33]] [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1467|24:27]].
