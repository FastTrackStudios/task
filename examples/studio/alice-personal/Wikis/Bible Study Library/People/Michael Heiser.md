# Michael Heiser

Old Testament scholar (1963–2023) whose *The Unseen Realm* (2015) and *Reversing Hermon* (2017) set out the reading this wiki’s conquest pages follow: the [[Divine Council]], the sons of God of [[Genesis 6]] as divine beings, the nations allotted at Babel ([[Deuteronomy 32]]), and the geography of [[Herem|ḥērem]] matching the Anakim ([[Nephilim and Anakim]]).

His 2004 University of Wisconsin–Madison dissertation, *The Divine Council in Late Canonical and Non-Canonical Second Temple Jewish Literature*, drew together the work that followed [[Ugarit]] — Robinson, Cross, Mullen, Smith — and argued that the council was not an early belief Israel later dropped but runs through Daniel, the Dead Sea Scrolls and the New Testament [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t362|6:02]].

His summary of [[Elohim]] is the line both videos lean on: **Yahweh is an elohim, but no other elohim is Yahweh** [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t592|9:52]]. The larger story he told from it — [[Three Rebellions]], the [[Principalities and Powers]], Pentecost as [[Reversing Babel]] — is [[The Inheritance of the Nations]].

Both videos behind these pages ([[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b]] and [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf]]) follow him closely. Heiser’s framework is influential in evangelical scholarship and disputed at several points, chiefly the Genesis 6 reading; pages that depend on it say so.
