# Elohim

English Bibles nearly always render *elohim* as “God” — and for Yahweh, over two thousand times from [[bible::Gen.1.1|Genesis 1:1]] on, that is right. But the word is not a name. It is a category, and the Hebrew Bible puts several different kinds of being in it [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t409|6:49]].

## Who is called elohim

| Who | Where |
|---|---|
| Yahweh, the God of Israel | [[bible::Gen.1.1\|Genesis 1:1]] and throughout |
| The members of the divine council | [[bible::Ps.82.1\|Psalm 82:1]], [[bible::Ps.82.6\|Psalm 82:6]] |
| The sons of God (*bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm*) | [[bible::Job.1.6\|Job 1:6]] |
| The gods of the nations — Chemosh, Dagon, Baal | [[bible::Judg.11.24\|Judges 11:24]] |
| The spirit of the dead Samuel | [[bible::1Sam.28.13\|1 Samuel 28:13]] |

What they share is where they live, not what they are: *elohim* is, in Michael Heiser’s phrase, a place-of-residence term for anything whose home is the spiritual realm rather than the embodied one [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t455|7:35]]. Samuel is not God; he is a disembodied dead man, and that puts him in the category [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t500|8:20]].

## “God” means more to us than it did to them

When we say *God* we mean omniscient, omnipotent, uncreated. The biblical writers did not load the word that way — which is why they can call other beings *elohim* and, in the same books, deny those beings the qualities that belong to Yahweh [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t546|9:06]].

So the uniqueness claim is not “there is only one *elohim*” but “there is only one Yahweh”. Heiser’s summary, quoted twice in the video: **Yahweh is an elohim, but no other elohim is Yahweh** [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t592|9:52]]. He is not first among equals; he made the others and judges them.
