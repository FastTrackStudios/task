# Ugarit

A Bronze Age Canaanite port city on the Syrian coast, destroyed around 1200 BC and buried until 1928. Its tablets are the nearest thing we have to the religious world of Israel’s neighbours in their own words, and they are why scholars now read the [[Divine Council]] as literal.

## The find

```timeline
1928 | A farmer ploughing near the harbour of Minet el-Beida breaks into a tomb of Bronze Age objects [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t181|3:01]].
1928 | René Dussaud at the Louvre judges them too fine for a village; the French dig moves inland to the mound of Ras Shamra, under Claude Schaeffer [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t226|3:46]].
1929 | The first tablets, in an unknown alphabetic cuneiform of about thirty signs. (The video says 1927 — a slip: the dig began in 1929.)
1930 | Hans Bauer, Édouard Dhorme and Charles Virolleaud, working separately, decipher it within months [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t271|4:31]].
```

## What the tablets say

Among the myths is the Baal Cycle. Its heaven is an assembly: El, the aged father, presides; lesser gods receive domains to rule and can contest for power; together they decide how the world is run [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t271|4:31]]. El’s council meets on a mountain, Zaphon, in the north.

Readers of the Hebrew Bible recognised it at once — the imagery, the vocabulary and the structure [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t316|5:16]]:

- “The assembly of El” is the phrase in [[bible::Ps.82.1|Psalm 82:1]] (see [[Psalm 82]]).
- “The mount of assembly in the far reaches of the north” in [[bible::Isa.14.13|Isaiah 14:13]] is Zaphon [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1180|19:40]].
- Ugaritic is a sister language of Hebrew, so it also clears up Hebrew words and idioms that had been obscure.

The Bible borrows the frame, not the theology. Yahweh is not El renamed: El created nothing, and Yahweh created the council he presides over [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t637|10:37]]. The same pattern — shared form, pointed difference — is the argument of [[The Kirta Epic]], another Ugaritic text.

## The scholarship it started

```timeline
1944 | H. Wheeler Robinson, “The Council of Yahweh” — the council is meant literally, not as a metaphor.
1973 | Frank Moore Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic*.
1980 | E. Theodore Mullen, *The Assembly of the Gods*.
2001 | Mark S. Smith, *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism*.
2004 | [[Michael Heiser]], dissertation on the divine council in Second Temple literature.
```

Heiser’s contribution, as the video puts it, was continuity: the council was not an early belief Israel later dropped, but runs through Daniel, the Dead Sea Scrolls and the New Testament [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t362|6:02]].
