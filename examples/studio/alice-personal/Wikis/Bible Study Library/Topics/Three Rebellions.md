# Three Rebellions

In Heiser’s reading, Genesis 3–11 is three rebellions by members of the [[Divine Council]], each worse than the one before: one being, then a group, then a whole tier. Everything after Genesis 11 is God’s campaign to undo them [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2094|34:54]].

## At a glance

| | Who | What they did | The answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The serpent — one council member | Corrupts humanity inside the sacred space; death enters | [[bible::Gen.3.15\|Genesis 3:15]]: a seed will crush its head |
| 2 | The sons of God, the watchers | Cross into the human world, father the Nephilim, teach forbidden arts | The flood; the watchers chained until judgment |
| 3 | The sons of God given the nations | Take the nations for themselves after Babel and take their worship | [[Psalm 82]]: sentenced to die; God will inherit the nations |

## 1. Eden

The [[Nachash|nāḥāš]] of [[bible::Gen.3.1|Genesis 3:1]] is not a garden snake. The word can mean serpent, diviner, or shining one; the creature knows what God said and what eating would mean, and it is inside God’s space — a council member who should have guarded it [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2140|35:40]].

Its curse carries the first promise:

[[bible::Gen.3.15|Genesis 3:15]] — Enmity between the serpent’s seed and the woman’s, and a crushed head: the promise the rest of the Bible keeps.

The Bible takes the rest of its length to resolve it — the powers disarmed in [[bible::Col.2.15|Colossians 2:15]], the ancient serpent bound and thrown down in [[bible::Rev.20.2|Revelation 20:2]] [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2186|36:26]].

(The triple meaning of *nāḥāš* is Heiser’s argument and is not widely shared; the case for a divine being here does not depend on it.)

## 2. The sons of God

[[bible::Gen.6.1-Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:1–4]]: sons of God take human wives, and the Nephilim are born. These are not the same beings as the ones later given the nations [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2232|37:12]]. The story, its reception, and what became of the Nephilim are on [[Genesis 6]], [[Watchers and Demons]] and [[Nephilim and Anakim]].

## 3. Babel

[[bible::Gen.11.1-Gen.11.9|Genesis 11:1–9]]: a city and a tower “with its top in the heavens” — most likely a ziggurat, a temple built to bring the gods down to it [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2458|40:58]]. God scatters them, languages divide, and the nations are born; [[bible::Deut.32.8-Deut.32.9|Deuteronomy 32:8–9]] says he gave them to the sons of God and kept Israel [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2504|41:44]].

This is the longest storyline in the Old Testament. The beings given the nations became their gods and took their worship; Paul calls them the [[Principalities and Powers]]. Israel’s wars, Elijah on Carmel and Daniel in Babylon all happen on top of that conflict [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2550|42:30]].

## The undoing

The same three, answered in order: Abraham called out of Babel’s world, Israel kept as Yahweh’s own, the Son of Man given the nations, the powers disarmed at the cross, Babel reversed at Pentecost ([[Reversing Babel]]), and the serpent defeated at the end [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3420|57:00]]. See [[The Inheritance of the Nations]].

## Two tellings in this wiki

The first source counts Eden as humanity’s rebellion ([[Divine Council]]); this one counts it as the serpent’s. They agree about Genesis 6 and Babel, and both follow Heiser’s *The Unseen Realm*.
