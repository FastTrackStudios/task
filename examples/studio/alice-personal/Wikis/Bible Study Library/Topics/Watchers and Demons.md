# Watchers and Demons

The second of the [[Three Rebellions]] as Second Temple Judaism told it, and where, on that telling, demons come from.

## Who the sons of God in Genesis 6 were

## The watchers

*1 Enoch* — among the most widely read Jewish books of the period — fills in the story: about two hundred “watchers”, led by Shemihazah, descend on Mount Hermon, take wives, father giants, and teach humanity what it should not know: metalworking for weapons, sorcery [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2277|37:57]]. They are judged and imprisoned.

The New Testament assumes its readers know this [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2322|38:42]]:

- [[bible::Jude.1.6|Jude 6]] — angels who left their proper dwelling, kept in eternal chains under gloomy darkness until the judgment. (Jude goes on to quote *1 Enoch* by name in verses 14–15.)
- [[bible::2Pet.2.4|2 Peter 2:4]] — angels who sinned, cast into Tartarus and committed to chains of gloomy darkness.

“Watcher” is also a word in Daniel: a decree “by the decision of the watchers” ([[bible::Dan.4.17|Daniel 4:17]]).

## Where demons come from

The watchers themselves are chained, not loose. Their children were not so lucky: the giants died — in the flood, and later in the wars against the Anakim ([[Nephilim and Anakim]]) — and the tradition *1 Enoch* develops is that their spirits outlived their bodies. Those disembodied spirits are what the Gospels call demons or unclean spirits: beings looking for a body to live in [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2367|39:27]].

That makes three distinct kinds of being which English tends to lump together as “fallen angels” [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2412|40:12]]:

| | What they are | Where they are |
|---|---|---|
| The watchers | Sons of God who sinned before the flood | Bound until the judgment |
| The rulers of the nations | Sons of God given the nations after Babel, who took them | Still ruling — Paul’s [[Principalities and Powers]] |
| Demons | The spirits of the dead Nephilim | Roaming, seeking bodies |

> [!question]- How firm is this?
> The watcher story is well attested as what Jews of Jesus’ day believed, and Jude and 2 Peter plainly draw on it. The origin of demons in the Nephilim is *1 Enoch*’s (chapter 15) and is never stated in the Bible itself; the video presents it as the “widespread Second Temple tradition”, which is fair, not as scripture.
