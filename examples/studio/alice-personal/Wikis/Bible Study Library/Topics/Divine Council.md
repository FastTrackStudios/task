# The Divine Council

The Old Testament’s world is not God and humanity and nothing else. It is populated by [[Elohim|elohim]] — a category word for God, the gods of the nations, angels and other spiritual beings — arranged as a heavenly government with Yahweh over it [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1284|21:24]].

The model is a royal court: the king rules alone, his sons hold the high offices, messengers carry out the errands [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1089|18:09]]. Its closest ancient parallel is the assembly of El in the tablets from [[Ugarit]] — which is how scholars came to read the biblical council literally.

## Three tiers

| Tier | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yahweh | Creator and judge of the rest; not first among equals but a different kind of being |
| 2 | The sons of God (*bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm*) | Govern — the nations were allotted to them ([[Deuteronomy 32]]); Paul’s [[Principalities and Powers]] |
| 3 | The messengers ([[Malak|malʾākîm]]) — angels | Carry specific errands into the human world: the visitors to Abraham, Gabriel to Daniel |

This is Heiser’s synthesis of the biblical and Ugaritic evidence [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t637|10:37]] [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t682|11:22]]. The word that covers all of them is [[Elohim]].

God does not need counsellors; he includes them, the way he includes people [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1044|17:24]]. These are beings with real power and the ability to rebel.

## The mountain

The council meets on a mountain. El’s met on Zaphon, in the north; [[bible::Isa.14.13|Isaiah 14:13]]’s would-be usurper says “I will sit on the mount of assembly in the far reaches of the north”. So Moses climbing Sinai is going where the council meets [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1180|19:40]] [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1226|20:26]].

## Why it matters for the conquest

Israel enters a land whose nations are under hostile powers and whose hill country is held by the Anakim — on this reading, the earthly remnant of the second rebellion [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1652|27:32]]. See [[Nephilim and Anakim]].

The council is also the model for how God works: not needing helpers, but involving them — as he involves Israel in the conquest [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t3016|50:16]].
