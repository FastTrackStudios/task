# What the Bible Really Says About the Other Gods

Nils Glenn · 61 min · a video essay on the [[Divine Council]], read from [[Psalm 82]] outward to the whole Bible.

## Overview

The video’s claim is that [[bible::Ps.82.8|Psalm 82:8]] — “Arise, O God, judge the earth, for you shall inherit all the nations” — is the thesis statement of the Bible. To see it you need the council the psalm describes: Yahweh presiding over lesser [[Elohim|elohim]], some of whom were given the nations and ruled them badly.

It builds that council from the Ugaritic tablets and the scholarship that followed them ([[Ugarit]]), from the word [[Elohim]] as a category rather than a name, and from the council scenes in Job, 1 Kings, Isaiah and Daniel. It then argues this is not polytheism ([[Does the Bible teach that other gods exist]]), tells the story of [[Three Rebellions]] — the serpent, the [[Watchers and Demons|watchers]], and Babel — and follows the reversal through the New Testament: [[Principalities and Powers]] disarmed at the cross, Babel undone at Pentecost and in Paul’s westward mission ([[Reversing Babel]]), the Son of Man of [[Daniel 7]] receiving the nations, and Revelation’s healed nations. [[The Inheritance of the Nations]] follows that thread end to end.

It is the same speaker as [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b]], and it leans on [[Michael Heiser]] throughout; this one is the framework the conquest video only sketched.

### Psalm 82 as the thesis [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t0|0:00]]

God stands in the divine assembly and judges the *elohim*; they are sentenced to die like men; the psalm ends asking God to inherit the nations. That last verse, the video says, is what the rest of the Bible answers. See [[Psalm 82]].

### Ugarit [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t181|3:01]]

A farmer’s plough opens a tomb near Minet el-Beida in 1928; the dig moves to Ras Shamra; the tablets are an unknown alphabetic cuneiform, deciphered by 1930. Their myths describe El presiding over an assembly of gods — and biblical scholars recognise it. See [[Ugarit]].

### Elohim is a category [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t409|6:49]]

*Elohim* names a place of residence, not a nature: Yahweh, the gods of the nations, the sons of God, even the dead Samuel. “Yahweh is an elohim, but no other elohim is Yahweh.” See [[Elohim]].

### The council’s three tiers [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t637|10:37]]

Yahweh; the sons of God, who govern the nations; the [[Malak|malʾākîm]], messengers. See [[Divine Council]].
