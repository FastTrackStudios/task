# Deuteronomy 32

The Song of Moses. Verses 8–9 ([[bible::Deut.32.8-Deut.32.9|Deuteronomy 32:8–9]]) are the ones that matter here.

## The textual variant

When the Most High divided the nations, he fixed their borders “according to the number of the ___”:

| Witness | Reading |
|---|---|
| Masoretic Text (most English Bibles until recently) | sons of **Israel** |
| Dead Sea Scrolls (4QDeut<sup>j</sup>) | sons of **God** |
| Septuagint | angels of God (some manuscripts: sons of God) |

Most textual critics now take “sons of God” as older; the ESV and NRSV follow it. “Sons of Israel” is hard to make sense of at Babel, before Israel existed, and looks like a scribal change to avoid the theology of the original [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1560|26:00]].

## What it says

After Babel ([[bible::Gen.11.1-Gen.11.9|Genesis 11:1–9]]) the nations — seventy of them in the table of [[bible::Gen.10.1|Genesis 10:1]] — are allotted to members of the [[Divine Council]], “but the LORD’s portion is his people”. Those beings exploit rather than shepherd their nations, and [[bible::Ps.82.1-Ps.82.8|Psalm 82:1–8]] is their sentence [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1605|26:45]].

The Qumran manuscript, 4QDeutʲ, came out of Cave 4 in 1952; the Septuagint, translated centuries before the Masoretic manuscripts were copied, preserves the same sense independently [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1273|21:13]].

## What it sets up

Verse 9 is the other half: “the LORD’s portion is his people, Jacob his allotted heritage.” Every other nation got a son of God; Israel got Yahweh directly [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1365|22:45]]. That is the frame for the Old Testament’s whole relation between Israel and the nations: the gods of Canaan, Egypt, Assyria and Babylon are, on this reading, real beings with real — and abused — authority [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1410|23:30]].

This is the third of the [[Three Rebellions]]. Its reversal — the nations taken back — is [[The Inheritance of the Nations]]; in Paul the same beings are the [[Principalities and Powers]].
