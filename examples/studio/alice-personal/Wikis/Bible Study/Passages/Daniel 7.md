# Daniel 7

Four beasts rise from the sea; then the scene moves to heaven, and a court sits.

## The court

“As I looked, thrones were placed, and the Ancient of Days took his seat” ([[bible::Dan.7.9|Daniel 7:9]]). *Thrones*, plural: this is the [[Divine Council]] in session. “The court sat in judgment, and the books were opened” ([[bible::Dan.7.10|Daniel 7:10]]) [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1868|31:08]].

## The Son of Man

One like a son of man comes with the clouds of heaven to the Ancient of Days and is given dominion, glory and a kingdom, “that all peoples, nations and languages should serve him” ([[bible::Dan.7.13-Dan.7.14|Daniel 7:13–14]]).

*Peoples, nations and languages* is Babel’s vocabulary. The nations divided there and handed to the sons of God ([[Deuteronomy 32]]), the nations [[Psalm 82]] asks God to inherit, are here given to one figure [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3282|54:42]].

## Jesus claims it

Asked by the high priest whether he is the Christ, Jesus answers: “I am, and you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power, and coming with the clouds of heaven” ([[bible::Mark.14.62|Mark 14:62]]) — Daniel 7, applied to himself, before the council that condemns him [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t1913|31:53]].

The video pairs this with Jesus’ use of Psalm 82 in [[bible::John.10.34|John 10:34]]: the council’s members were called [[Elohim|elohim]], and he is the heir the council was pointing to [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3282|54:42]].

## After Daniel

In [[bible::Rev.5.1-Rev.5.14|Revelation 5]] no one in heaven or on earth is worthy to open the scroll until the Lamb, who has conquered, takes it — and the song is about every tribe, language, people and nation ([[bible::Rev.5.9|Revelation 5:9]]) [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3330|55:30]].

> [!question]- Other readings
> In Daniel itself the “one like a son of man” is interpreted as “the saints of the Most High” ([[bible::Dan.7.18|Daniel 7:18]], [[bible::Dan.7.27|Daniel 7:27]]), and many scholars read the figure as Israel, or as its angelic prince (Michael), rather than an individual messiah. The Gospels’ individual reading is how the New Testament uses it; both are in the text.
