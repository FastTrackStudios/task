# Psalm 82

Eight verses, and the passage the whole [[Divine Council]] reading turns on: God stands in the assembly, charges the gods, sentences them, and the psalm ends asking for the nations.

## The trial, verse by verse

| Verses | What happens |
|---|---|

| [[bible::Ps.82.2-Ps.82.4\|Psalm 82:2–4]] | The charge: they judge unjustly, favour the wicked, and fail the weak, the fatherless and the needy. |

## Who are the “gods”?

## Human judges
Israel’s rulers, called gods because they judged in God’s name.
+ Fits the charge: unjust judgment and partiality to the wicked (verses 2–4).
+ A long history in Christian reading, and how many read [[bible::John.10.34|John 10:34]].

## Divine beings
The council of [[Elohim|elohim]] given the nations in [[Deuteronomy 32]], judged for misruling them.
+ “The assembly of El” in verse 1 is the phrase [[Ugarit]] uses for the council of the gods.
+ It explains why the psalm ends asking for the nations.
- It makes the Old Testament’s heaven more crowded than many readers expect.

## Jesus quotes it

Accused of blasphemy for “I and the Father are one”, Jesus answers with verse 6: “Is it not written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?” ([[bible::John.10.34-John.10.36|John 10:34–36]]). The argument runs from lesser to greater: if scripture calls beings *elohim*, how much more the one the Father consecrated and sent. The video argues this only works if the *elohim* are real divine beings; a claim that “we are all gods” would not defend his own identity [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3191|53:11]].

## The last verse

“You shall inherit all the nations” is, on this reading, the prayer the rest of the Bible answers: [[Daniel 7]]’s Son of Man receives the nations, Pentecost addresses them in their own languages, and Revelation’s nations are healed. See [[The Inheritance of the Nations]].
