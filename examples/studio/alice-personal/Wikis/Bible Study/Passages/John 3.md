# John 3

A night conversation between Jesus and a Pharisee named Nicodemus,
followed by a section of commentary whose speaker the text does not
mark — one of the reasons translations disagree about where the quotation
ends.

## Setting

Nicodemus comes at night ([[bible::John.3.1]]). Readers have taken the
detail two ways: as caution, since he is a member of the ruling council,
or simply as when rabbis studied. The text does not say, and both
readings have a long history.

## The famous verse

[[bible::John.3.16]] is quoted more than any other verse in the canon,
which has worn the edges off it. Read in place it is the middle of an
argument rather than a summary of one, and it follows a comparison to
the bronze serpent in Numbers that most quotations drop.

## Note on anchoring

The references above name verses, not text. Nothing on this page
reproduces the Resource, so the page works whichever translation the
reader has installed and does not go stale when a different edition is
added.
