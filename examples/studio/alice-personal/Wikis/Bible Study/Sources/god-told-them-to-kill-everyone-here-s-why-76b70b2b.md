# God Told Them to Kill Everyone. Here’s Why.

Nils Glenn · 52 min · a video essay on the [[Herem|ḥērem]] (“devote to destruction”) commands in Deuteronomy and Joshua.

## Overview

The video answers the objection that the God of the Old Testament commanded genocide. Its case has five moves: the conquest is more historical than the “it’s all myth” position allows but less total than a surface reading suggests; most conquest verbs mean *dispossess*, not *kill*; the killing language clusters in the hill country where the Anakim lived; the Anakim belong to a cosmic conflict that begins in [[bible::Gen.6.1-Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:1–4]]; and “leave none alive” is the ordinary idiom of ancient victory reports. It ends by tracing the Anakim who escaped to Gath through to Goliath and to [[bible::2Sam.21.22|2 Samuel 21:22]].

The argument follows Michael Heiser’s *The Unseen Realm* closely (see [[Michael Heiser]]) and draws its rhetoric point from [[K. Lawson Younger]]. See [[Did God command genocide in Canaan]] for the question the wiki builds around it.

## Chapters

### The moral problem [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t0|0:00]]

The objection as it is usually put: the Old Testament God is angry and violent, the New Testament God is love. Dawkins’ list of adjectives is read out [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t91|1:31]]. The claim of the video is that both loud sides are working from an incomplete picture [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t137|2:17]].

### Historicity and archaeology [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t274|4:34]]

Critics cannot treat the conquest as both myth and atrocity at once. Joshua burns only three cities; the rest are occupied, not razed. The Exodus numbers may count units rather than people. Jericho’s decisive layer is disturbed, and Hazor’s 13th-century destruction is real. See [[Historicity of the Conquest]].

### Analyzing the conquest commands [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t820|13:40]]

Most of the verbs are displacement verbs. The intermarriage ban beside the destruction command assumes survivors. Rahab and the Gibeonites survive. See [[Verbs of Conquest]].

### The hill country and the Anakim [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1096|18:16]]

Every *ḥērem* location is elevated terrain — the same map as the Anakim of [[bible::Num.13.33|Numbers 13:33]]. See [[Nephilim and Anakim]].

### Cosmic rebellion [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1284|21:24]]

The divine council, the sons of God in Genesis 6, and the nations allotted to divine beings at Babel ([[bible::Deut.32.8-Deut.32.9|Deuteronomy 32:8–9]]). See [[Divine Council]] and [[Genesis 6]].

### Ḥērem [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1697|28:17]]

A consecration word before it is a violence word: taken out of human use and handed to God. Achan’s theft is the illustration. See [[Herem]].

### Literary conventions of war [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1879|31:19]]

“None left alive” is how Egyptian, Assyrian and Hittite kings reported decisive wins; Joshua itself records remnants in the same breath. See [[Conquest Rhetoric]].

### Parallels with the Kirta epic [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2198|36:38]]

A Ugaritic king’s campaign shares the two-week, seventh-day shape of Joshua 1–6, and the differences read as a polemic. See [[The Kirta Epic]].

### From Joshua to David [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2744|45:44]]

The Anakim who fled to Gath, Goliath, and the four giants of 2 Samuel 21 — and a closing turn to the powers of Ephesians and the resurrection. See [[From Genesis 6 to Goliath]].
