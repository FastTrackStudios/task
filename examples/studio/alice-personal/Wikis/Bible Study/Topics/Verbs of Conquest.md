# Verbs of Conquest

The popular picture is a single kill order. The texts use several verbs, and most of them describe something else [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t866|14:26]].

| Hebrew | Sense | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| [[Yarash|yārash]] | dispossess, take possession, drive out | displacement |
| *gārash* | drive out, expel | displacement |
| *nāšal* | clear away ([[bible::Deut.7.1\|Deuteronomy 7:1]]) | displacement |
| *nākāh* | strike | killing |
| *šāmad* | destroy | killing |
| *ḥāram* | devote to God — see [[Herem]] | consecration / killing |

Displacement dominates. God “will send hornets” ahead of Israel to drive peoples out ([[bible::Exod.23.28|Exodus 23:28]]) — a migration, not a massacre [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t957|15:57]].

## The texts assume survivors

[[bible::Deut.7.2-Deut.7.3|Deuteronomy 7:2–3]] puts “devote them to destruction” and “do not intermarry with them” two verses apart. A marriage law for a people who are all dead makes no sense; the law presupposes survivors [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1004|16:44]].

And there are survivors in the narrative:

- [[Rahab]] and her whole family are spared at Jericho ([[bible::Josh.6.25|Joshua 6:25]]).
- The Gibeonites win a treaty by deception; Joshua honours it anyway, and they end up serving at the sanctuary ([[bible::Josh.9.27|Joshua 9:27]], [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1050|17:30]]).
