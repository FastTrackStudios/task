# Nephilim and Anakim

The claim at the centre of the video: **the killing commands and the giants are on the same map** [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1096|18:16]].

## The map

Where [[Herem|ḥērem]] is carried out, the terrain is high: Hebron and the Judean highlands, Debir and Anab south of Hebron, Jerusalem on its ridge, Ai in the central highlands, Hazor in the Galilean hills (Lachish, in the foothills, is the edge case). The coastal plain and the valleys get the displacement verbs [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1142|19:02]].

Where the Anakim live, the text says: the hill country — Hebron, Debir, Anab ([[bible::Josh.11.21|Joshua 11:21]]; [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1236|20:36]]). It is the same map.

## Who the Anakim are

The spies of [[bible::Num.13.28|Numbers 13:28]] saw “the descendants of Anak”, and in [[bible::Num.13.33|Numbers 13:33]] call them Nephilim: “we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers”. Their report cost a generation forty years in the wilderness [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1189|19:49]].

The Nephilim first appear in [[Genesis 6]], born of the sons of God and human women, “on the earth in those days, and also afterward” ([[bible::Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:4]]). The Anakim are, in the text’s own words, their descendants.

## What this does to the question

On this reading *ḥērem* is aimed at a specific people in a specific place — the surviving arm of a rebellion against God, holding the land he is giving to Israel — rather than at Canaanites as such [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t1652|27:32]]. Joshua’s own summary of success is not “no Canaanites left” but “none of the Anakim left”, except in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod ([[bible::Josh.11.22|Joshua 11:22]]). See [[Joshua 11]].

## After death

Second Temple tradition has one more chapter: when the giants died, their spirits did not, and those disembodied spirits are the demons of the Gospels [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2367|39:27]]. See [[Watchers and Demons]].

> [!question]- Contested
> The geography is Michael Heiser’s argument (*The Unseen Realm*), and it rests on reading the sons of God as divine beings. Many interpreters read [[bible::Gen.6.1-Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:1–4]] as the line of Seth, or as kings, and the Anakim as simply tall people; on those readings the map stays interesting but loses its explanation. See [[Michael Heiser]].
