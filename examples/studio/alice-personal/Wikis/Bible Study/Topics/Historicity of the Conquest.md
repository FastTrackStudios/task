# Historicity of the Conquest

Whether the conquest happened matters to whether it was right: a critic cannot hold both that the story is later propaganda and that it proves an atrocity [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t320|5:20]].

## What the text actually claims

Joshua burns three cities — Jericho ([[bible::Josh.6.24|Joshua 6:24]]), Ai ([[bible::Josh.8.28|Joshua 8:28]]) and Hazor ([[bible::Josh.11.13|Joshua 11:13]]), and says outright that Israel did *not* burn the other northern cities. The promise of [[bible::Deut.6.10-Deut.6.11|Deuteronomy 6:10–11]] is of cities “you did not build” and houses “full of all good things”: standing and furnished. So the expected evidence is a change of population and material culture, not a burn layer across Canaan [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t411|6:51]].

## The numbers

Read as a headcount, the Exodus figures give two to three million people. The Hebrew *ʾelep* can also mean a clan unit or a military contingent; read that way, estimates fall to 25,000–200,000 [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t456|7:36]]. A group that size leaves little trace in the Sinai after three thousand years.

## Absence of evidence

Egypt fought constantly in Canaan in this period; almost none of those campaigns has left physical evidence either. Excavation is partial and sites are rebuilt on [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t549|9:09]].

## Jericho

Kathleen Kenyon (1950s) dated the main destruction to about 1550 BC, too early for Joshua on most chronologies. Lorenzo Nigro’s later excavations found the site reoccupied after that, a structure that disappears in the 13th century, and a village in the 12th — and the layer that would settle the question cut through by Iron Age and Roman building [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t639|10:39]]. Consistent with Joshua 6; not a confirmation of it.

## Hazor

The largest Late Bronze Age city in Canaan, “the head of all those kingdoms” ([[bible::Josh.11.10|Joshua 11:10]]), has an unambiguous 13th-century destruction: palace burned, cult statues deliberately defaced [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t730|12:10]].

**Contested:** the video calls Israelite responsibility the mainstream consensus. Its excavator Amnon Ben-Tor argues for it; others propose an internal revolt or another attacker. Treat it as a strong case, not a settled one.

## Verdict

Archaeology has not confirmed every detail of Joshua and cannot. It also does not show the “it never happened” picture the popular skeptical case claims.
