## The footnote that waits four centuries

Joshua declares the conquest complete and mentions, almost in passing, that some Anakim fled to three Philistine cities on the coast [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2789|46:29]]. Judges, Ruth and the early chapters of Samuel go by. Then a man from Gath — one of those three cities — challenges Israel [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2834|47:14]].

David is, in this reading, the new Joshua: he walks into the part of the mission Joshua left unfinished. His men then hunt down Goliath’s kin one by one, and [[bible::2Sam.21.22|2 Samuel 21:22]] closes the account [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2925|48:45]].

## And past David

The giants are gone, but the powers behind the nations are not — the “rulers and authorities” of [[bible::Eph.6.12|Ephesians 6:12]]. They are disarmed not by a warrior but by a crucified man who rises ([[bible::Col.2.15|Colossians 2:15]]; [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t3062|51:02]]). The last giant fell to a shepherd’s stone; death falls to a death.
