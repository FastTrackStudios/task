# Reversing Babel

At Babel the nations were scattered, their languages confused, and — by [[Deuteronomy 32]] — each put under a son of God. The New Testament tells the undoing in the same terms: languages at Pentecost, nations on Paul’s map.

## Pentecost

The Spirit falls, the disciples speak in known languages, and Luke lists who heard ([[bible::Acts.2.9-Acts.2.11|Acts 2:9–11]]): Parthians, Medes, Elamites, Mesopotamia, Judea, Cappadocia, Pontus, Asia, Phrygia, Pamphylia, Egypt, Libya, Cyrene, Rome, Crete, Arabia [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2780|46:20]].

Set that beside the table of nations in [[bible::Gen.10.1-Gen.10.32|Genesis 10]]. At Babel one language became many and the peoples went apart; at Pentecost the many languages carry one message to the peoples gathered back together [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2827|47:07]].

## Paul’s route

If the nations are under corrupted powers, preaching to them is reclaiming territory, and Paul’s journeys read like a plan [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2874|47:54]]:

Antioch | 36.20, 36.16 | Syrian Antioch, the sending church
Salamis | 35.18, 33.90 | Cyprus

Lystra | 37.58, 32.45

Tarshish | 37.26, -6.95 | Tartessus in southern Spain — the edge of the known world
route: Jerusalem > Antioch > Salamis > Pisidian Antioch > Lystra > Ephesus > Troas > Philippi > Thessalonica > Corinth > Rome
route: Rome > Tarshish

Antioch in Syria, near Babel’s own world; Cyprus and Galatia in central Anatolia; Asia; then west into Greece and on to Rome — and beyond it, Spain.

The westward turn is steered: the Spirit blocks Asia and Bithynia, and a man of Macedonia calls him over ([[bible::Acts.16.6-Acts.16.10|Acts 16:6–10]]) [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2920|48:40]]. The peoples west and north-west — Anatolia, the Aegean, Europe — are Japheth’s line in Genesis 10.

## To Spain, which is Tarshish

Paul states the plan himself in [[bible::Rom.15.19-Rom.15.24|Romans 15:19–24]]: from Jerusalem around to Illyricum (the Balkans’ Adriatic coast) he has “fulfilled the gospel”, has no more room in these regions, and means to go to Spain by way of Rome — repeated in [[bible::Rom.15.28|Romans 15:28]] [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t2965|49:25]].

Why Spain? In the Hebrew Bible Tarshish is the far west. It is a son of Javan (Greece) in [[bible::Gen.10.4|Genesis 10:4]]; it is where Jonah runs to get away from God ([[bible::Jonah.1.3|Jonah 1:3]]); and in [[bible::Isa.66.19|Isaiah 66:19]] God sends survivors to Tarshish and “the coastlands far away that have not heard my fame” to declare his glory among the nations [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3010|50:10]]. Most identify it with Tartessus in southern Spain, though that is debated. For a Roman, Spain and the Pillars of Hercules were the edge of the world, with only the sea — the abyss — beyond [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3056|50:56]].

So “to Spain” means: finish the table of nations, to its end [[what-the-bible-really-says-about-the-other-gods-ac256dcf#^t3101|51:41]]. Whether he got there is uncertain (*1 Clement* says he reached “the limit of the west”); the point is what he took the mission to be.

> [!question]- How strong is this?
> The Pentecost–Babel echo is widely recognised. The claim that Paul planned his journeys around Genesis 10 is a scholarly thesis — associated especially with James M. Scott’s *Paul and the Nations* (1995), which Heiser followed — rather than a consensus, and a lot of the route is also just the Roman road network. Romans 15 is the strongest evidence for it.
