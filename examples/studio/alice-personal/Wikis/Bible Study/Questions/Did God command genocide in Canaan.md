# Did God command genocide in Canaan?

## The objection

In [[bible::Deut.20.16-Deut.20.17|Deuteronomy 20:16–17]] Israel is told to “save alive nothing that breathes” in the cities of Canaan, and Joshua reports doing so at Jericho ([[bible::Josh.6.21|Joshua 6:21]]) and elsewhere. The objection is that a God who commands this is not good — the “angry Old Testament God” set against the loving God of the New [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t0|0:00]].

The discomfort is the right place to start. A reader who feels nothing at these passages has not been paying attention.

## The answer this wiki is building

Five claims, each on its own page. None of them makes the passages comfortable; together they change what the passages are about.

| Claim | Page |
|---|---|
| The conquest has a historical core, but Joshua never says every city was destroyed. | [[Historicity of the Conquest]] |
| Most conquest verbs mean *dispossess*, not *kill*; the texts assume survivors. | [[Verbs of Conquest]] |
| The killing command clusters in the hill country, where the Anakim lived. | [[Nephilim and Anakim]] |
| *Ḥērem* is a consecration word — withdrawn from human use, handed to God. | [[Herem]] |
| “None left alive” is how ancient kings reported a decisive win. | [[Conquest Rhetoric]] |

Behind the third claim sits a worldview most modern readers have not met: the [[Divine Council]], the sons of God of [[Genesis 6]], and a conflict that runs [[From Genesis 6 to Goliath]].

## Where it is contested

- **The rhetoric argument** is widely held (Younger, Copan, Walton) but does not by itself remove the problem: even hyperbole describes a real war, and the command is still given.
- **The Anakim argument** depends on reading [[bible::Gen.6.1-Gen.6.4|Genesis 6:1–4]] as divine beings and on the Anakim being their descendants — the ancient Jewish reading, and Heiser’s, but not every commentator’s.
- **The archaeology** is genuinely open. Jericho’s decisive layer is missing; Hazor’s destruction is real but its attacker is debated.
- **Canaanite practice** (child sacrifice, attacks on the weak) is raised briefly in the video [[god-told-them-to-kill-everyone-here-s-why-76b70b2b#^t2153|35:53]] and deserves its own page with its own sources.
