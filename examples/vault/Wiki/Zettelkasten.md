# Zettelkasten

A note-taking method built around **atomic notes**: one idea per
note, written in your own words, linked to related notes by
explicit references. Originally developed by sociologist Niklas
Luhmann, who used a paper slip-box ("Zettelkasten") to produce
~70 books and 400 papers over his career.

The discipline is **distillation**: a fleeting note (raw capture)
gets read, rewritten as a single durable claim, and filed in
[[Wisdom]] with links to neighbors. The note's value is in the
links, not the content — a zettel without inbound or outbound
references is a dead zettel.

Differs from a [[Wiki]]: wiki entries are reference material
("what is X?"), zettels are personal claims ("X implies Y
because Z"). Both can coexist in the same vault.

Related: [[Spaced repetition]], [[Habit stacking]].
